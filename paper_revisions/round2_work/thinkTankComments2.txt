Reviewer #3: 1.Quotation marks around “fractal”The authors have revised the Discussion to use more specific wording, e.g., “self-similar architecture of the simulation.” However, the Introduction still retains: “Using a ‘fractal’ brain model, we developed software to convert any text string into a scalable-resolution, 3D model of neurometabolic activity based on ISR.” For consistency and precision, please either remove the quotation marks around “fractal” or explicitly define/justify the term at first use.2.Please supplement the experiments with a consistency analysis between the synthetic EEG and real EEG across the time-frequency domain, source space, and metabolic metrics.
Reviewer #4: The revised manuscript has substantially improved in clarity, methodological description, citation quality, and presentation. The authors have addressed many of the concerns raised in the previous round, including the characterization of the cognitive-linguistics background, the role of stress markers, the description of pseudorandom microgaps, software documentation, the role of the LLM, and several presentation issues. However, I do not think that the manuscript is yet technically ready for publication. My remaining concerns relate primarily to the validity and interpretation of the central results.1. The central biological interpretation is not sufficiently supported by the current experiment: The study demonstrates that inserting additional pseudorandom EEG segments between words changes the downstream simulated neurovascular variables. However, this does not demonstrate that the resulting signals are more biologically realistic or better reflect biological variability. The Abstract and Discussion should therefore avoid claims that the microgap strategy “enhances biological variability” unless this is validated against an independent biological reference, such as continuous imagined-speech EEG or natural pause data. The conclusions should instead be limited to the demonstrated finding that microgap insertion alters the simulated EEG-derived metabolic outputs. Relatedly, the workflow is primarily a text-to-EEG/metabolic simulation framework rather than reconstruction of unknown speech from EEG, and this distinction should be made clear.2. The statistical analysis and reported results require substantial reconsideration and verification: The manuscript treats the 16 EEG channels from a single participant as n = 16 observations, although these channels are spatially correlated and are not independent biological replicates. In addition, the reported neurovascular variables are mathematically derived from the same underlying gamma/CMRO2 signal and therefore should not be interpreted as independent evidence. Several tables also contain internally inconsistent values—for example, t = 0.00 together with p = 0.00 and Cohen’s d = 0.00—and some values differ unexpectedly from those in the original submission. The remaining p-value inconsistency between Trial 2 and the Abstract/Discussion should also be corrected. The authors should redefine the statistical unit of analysis, clarify the dependence among derived outcomes, and verify all tables directly against the underlying analysis.3. The quantitative results should be regenerated using the corrected computational pipeline: The public repository linked in the Data Availability statement documents several pipeline defects that affect the computed outputs, including repeated analysis of the same samples across trials, incorrect trial indexing, and incorrect distance weighting. The repository itself indicates that the manuscript tables, statistics, and supplementary figures should be regenerated after these corrections. Because these issues directly affect the numerical results supporting the manuscript, the corrected implementation should be used to rerun the complete analysis, and the manuscript, figures, tables, code release, and Data Availability statement should all refer to the same reproducible version.
